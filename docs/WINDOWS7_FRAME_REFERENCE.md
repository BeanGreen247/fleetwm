# Windows 7 window frame: reference numbers and what Fleetwm uses (2026-10-08)

Measured from two Wikimedia Commons screenshots of real Windows 7 windows (fetched with the browser skill, pixel-measured with PIL; the originals are not stored in the repo):
- `File:Aero UI.png` (192x158, Aero, unscaled): caption buttons hang from the top edge of the window under a 1 px dark outline; minimize about 26-27 px wide, maximize about 27 px, close about 47 px (red, glossy); button height about 19-20 px; about 7 px of glass between the close button and the window's right outer edge.
- `File:Kalkulačka.jpg` (499x336, Win7 calculator, probably scaled to about 90%): side and bottom frame about 7 px of glass between a 1 px outline and a 1 px inner highlight; top (caption plus frame) about 27 px; title text left-aligned after the application icon.
Unscaled Windows 7 metrics (from the Win32 system metrics, not re-measured here): frame 8 px (SM_CXSIZEFRAME 4 + padded border 4), caption about 22-23 px, so the top is about 30 px.

Fleetwm defaults after this change (`TitlebarConfig`, `theme.toml` values the user already has stay): titlebar height 30, buttons 28 x 20 hanging from the top edge, close button 47 px (`kStripCloseScale` 1.68), title left-aligned, frame 8 px (`frame_px`), outer corners radius 7. Checked in the nested rig at 1280x720 and 1024x768.

Not done: the application icon in front of the title; Aero glass/blur as the default (glass is a setting, off in the check above).

## Second pass: glass on by default, corners, outline (same day)
- Corner radius: the outline of the unscaled `Aero UI.png` bends over 3-4 px (from (156,32) to (159,35)), the calculator shot (about 90%) over 3-4 px of its outer highlight: Fleetwm's `kWindowCornerRadius` is 4 now (was 7).
- Frame: 8 px on the sides and bottom = 1 px outer light line + 6 px translucent fill + 1 px inner dark line (measured on the Fleetwm render: x 172..179 left, y 524..531 bottom). The titlebar is 30 px (rows 0..29), its last row is the inner dark line, the side strips start at row 30.
- The outer line used to stop at the titlebar's and the bottom strip's ends; both now carry the same 1 px line (same colour and alpha as the side strips), so the outline is continuous down the left and right edges (checked at 14x and 18x zoom on a render over a vivid wallpaper, 1280x720 and 1024x768).
- `glass_effects` defaults to true (was false); an existing `theme.toml` keeps its own value.

## Third pass: real desktop screenshots (same day)
Source: `File:Google Chrome Screenshot.png` (1066x725, unscaled Windows 7 Aero window incl. its glass frame, Wikimedia Commons, fetched through the Chrome debug browser and measured at 18x zoom). Findings that replaced the earlier guesses:
- The window edge is TWO lines that follow the rounded corners: a dark 1 px outline on the outside and a light 1 px line just inside it (left edge: x=13 dark, x=14 light, x=15..19 glass fill, x=20 inner light line, content from x=21: 8 px in all).
- The corner radius is about 6 px (the outline reaches the straight left edge about 6 px below the top and the top edge about 6 px to the right), on all four outer corners, for both lines. The previous version (radius 4, a straight light line clipped by the corner mask) looked like cut-off corners.
- Fleetwm now draws the outline with `kit::draw_window_edge` (radius `kWindowCornerRadius` = 6): dark line (black 60% on glass), light line inside it (white 60% on glass), in the titlebar (top corners), the side strips (straight) and the bottom strip (bottom corners), so the two lines run unbroken round the window.

## Fourth pass: colour, frost, caption glow, shadow (2026-10-20)
Source: `File:Hamilton C shell x64 on Windows 7.png` (1280x800, three Aero windows, focused and unfocused, taskbar; Wikimedia Commons, found through the Commons API with curl and measured at 4-6x zoom; also looked at SeaMonkey 2.46 and IE9 shots). The test scene is the same three-window layout over a blue wallpaper with light streaks (`glass-scene.sh` in the session scratch, not stored: a headless compositor, three `foot` windows, `grim`). What the real Windows 7 does that Fleetwm did not:
- **Caption text is dark with a wide soft white glow** (about 9 px reach), not light text. Now `kit::draw_text_glow` (fleetkit.cpp): the text shape blurred (3 box passes, radius 4) and boosted, kept per (text, size, weight) in a 512 KB cache, so a titlebar redraw is one masked fill. Used when the tint is an Aero colour; the "Theme colours" tint keeps the old light text.
- **The frame is a bright colour over the frosted wallpaper, not slate.** The tint engine used to mix the colour with the theme's dark background (every tint came out dark slate). `mix_glass_colors` now uses the colour itself (10% lightened), at 45-80% strength by the intensity slider for a focused window (`Palette::glass_alpha`) and 28-50% for an unfocused one, which is also greyer (`glass_alpha_idle`). Taskbar, menus and tooltips use the colour darkened by 42-22% (Windows 7's taskbar is the frame colour, deeper).
- **Frost under the frame.** The titlebar and frame strips were translucent over the raw wallpaper. The compositor now puts the blurred wallpaper under each of the four frame pieces (`View::frost_`, `update_glass_backdrop()`): one scene buffer per piece showing the part of the output-sized blurred wallpaper that lies under it, so the glass looks frosted and follows the window as it moves (source box update only, no drawing).
- **Two wide diagonal reflections** across the titlebar (brighter when focused) instead of one narrow band.
- **Drop shadow** (`window_shadows`, on by default, Settings -> Theme): a 24 px reach, darker and wider for the focused window, a little lower than the window; `src/fleetkit/window_shadow.*` draws one 80 px picture (a blurred rounded rectangle with the window's own area cleared) and the compositor cuts it into four 40 px corners and four 1 px edge strips stretched to the window size (`View::update_shadow`), so a window of any size and a window being dragged cost no new drawing. The shadow nodes take no clicks.
- Close button red is more saturated (0.84, 0.22, 0.15).
- **Light ray** (`kit::paint_glass_ray`, backdrop.cpp): one diagonal beam across each glass surface with a crisp bright leading edge, a hairline along it and a long soft tail, leaning like a "/" (the top is one bar height to the right of the bottom). Peak opacity 0.42 on a focused titlebar, 0.20 unfocused, 0.22 on the taskbar and other wide glass (`GlassStyle::ray`); it is drawn into the cached pictures, so it costs nothing per redraw.
- **Running-program line** under taskbar buttons (`taskbar_indicator` in bar.toml, default true): the Windows 7 taskbar has none, so it is not drawn while glass effects are on, and Settings -> Taskbar greys the switch out (shown off) until glass is turned off.
- The shadow was too dark and hard right under the window (a 20 px, 0.8 opacity picture): now 24 px reach, 0.55 (0.34 unfocused) opacity, a wider blur and 4 px lower.
Not done: Aero Peek, window open/close/minimise animations, the taskbar button hover glow that follows the pointer, a start orb.

## Fifth pass: the whole desktop, glass and Basic (2026-10-20)
Source: two full 1920x1080 Windows 7 screenshots from the owner (taskbar with orb, tiles, tray, clock and show-desktop sliver; Calculator unfocused, Purble Place and Messenger focused, Task Manager, About), measured pixel by pixel. What they showed and what Fleetwm does now (glass and no glass; both follow the tint, only "Theme colours" keeps the old flat theme look):
- **Frame edge, left side, measured:** outer dark line (10,31,41), outer light line (214,235,245), 4 px glass (52,157,207) over a (6,121,214) wallpaper, inner light line (166,212,233), inner dark line (37,81,102), then the client. Fleetwm draws the same five parts: `draw_frame_strip` and the titlebar now add the light line beside the dark one against the client area (the titlebar's bottom row too).
- **Colour:** the Aero tint is `#2d9ad0` lightened 6% at 45-80% strength over the frosted wallpaper (about (45,150,207) on a blue wallpaper); an unfocused frame is the colour mixed 62% to white at 20-42% strength, which gives the clearer, slightly lighter blue of the Calculator.
- **Basic (no glass):** opaque frame, a vertical gradient of the tint (58% to 30% toward white focused, a grey-blue unfocused), black caption text (never bold), same outlines and caption buttons (`basic_colors` in titlebar_draw.cpp); the taskbar is the tint darkened 40%-62% with a light line under the dark top edge (`kit::paint_basic_taskbar`).
- **Taskbar (`src/fleetkit/win7_look.*`, `src/bar/main.cpp`):** icon-only 52 px tiles (`kTileW`): every running program is a glass tile (1 px dark outline, 1 px light line inside, lighter top half), the focused one brighter, an idle pin shows no tile until the pointer is on it (then the light follows the pointer); a round start orb with a four-pane mark; a 14 px show-desktop sliver at the far end (click: IPC `SHOW_DESKTOP` minimizes every window or brings the same ones back); the clock is white; the dark/light edge lines; the running-program line is only drawn without glass and only if `taskbar_indicator` is on. `taskbar_labels` now defaults to off and the titlebar pin button to hidden, as on Windows 7.
- **Light ray:** `paint_glass_ray` strength 0.55 focused / 0.28 unfocused on titlebars, 0.34 on the taskbar; the caption glow reaches 14 px (radius 5).
Not done: Aero Peek thumbnails, window animations, the notification-area overflow arrow, grouping of several windows of one program into one tile, a start menu in the Windows 7 style.

## Sixth pass: how Windows 7 builds the glass, found with the browser (2026-10-20)
Sources, read with the browser skill (Playwright over CDP; search engines block headless browsers, so the pages were reached from Stack Overflow's API, its answers and the Internet Archive): Rafael Rivera, "Retrieving Aero Glass base color for opaque surface rendering" (Within Windows, 2010, via web.archive.org): DWM keeps seven colorization values, `ColorizationColor`, `ColorizationAfterglow`, `ColorizationColorBalance`, `ColorizationAfterglowBalance`, `ColorizationBlurBalance`, `ColorizationGlassReflectionIntensity`, `ColorizationOpaqueBlend` (`HKCU\Software\Microsoft\Windows\DWM`); and the open-source reimplementation Maplespe/DWMBlurGlass (`DWMBlurGlassExt/Backdrops/AeroBackdrop.hpp`, `GlassReflectionBackdrop.hpp`, `Common/Common.h`), which rebuilds Aero from DWM's own maths.
- **The recipe:** `out = blurBalance x blurred backdrop + afterglowBalance x grey(blurred backdrop) x afterglow colour + colorBalance x colour`, added together (D2D `PLUS`); the backdrop is a Gaussian blur of EVERYTHING behind the window, other windows included. An unfocused window uses `colorBalance x 0.4` and `blurBalance x 0.4 + 0.6` (more backdrop, less colour). Defaults in the project: colour balance 0.08, afterglow 0.43, blur 0.49.
- **The reflection:** a monitor-sized texture (`aeropeek.png`) anchored to the SCREEN: the brush offset is `-(window position - monitor position) x (1 - parallax)` with parallax 0.1, so the reflection stays put while a window moves, shifted 10% with the window (horizontal only), at `GlassReflectionIntensity` opacity.
- **Fit to the owner's screenshots:** with `frame = a x backdrop + m x Sky` on the measured pixels, a = 0.45 (focused), 0.67 (unfocused), 0.39 (taskbar) and the colour term lands on Windows 7's default colour Sky (116,184,252) x 0.857 at alpha 0.55 (focused), a lighter tint at 0.33 (unfocused), Sky x 0.75 at 0.61 (taskbar). That is what the code uses now: `aero_blue()` = `#74b8fc`; focused tint Sky x 0.857 at alpha 0.50, unfocused Sky lightened 15% at 0.30, taskbar Sky x 0.75 at 0.60 (all three scale with the intensity slider).
- **Blur radius:** Windows 7's blur is small, the wallpaper's shapes stay recognisable through the frame; the old backdrop (192 px wide, radius 5) flattened everything. It is now 480 px wide with three box passes of radius 3 (sigma about 14 px at 1920 wide).
- **Reflection in Fleetwm:** `glass_ray.cpp` keeps the ray picture per output size (a quarter scale, with a 12% margin each side); every window shows it through its four frame pieces (`View::ray_`) with the 10% parallax and 0.70 (focused) / 0.40 (unfocused) opacity; the taskbar uses the same beam at its screen position. Saturating the backdrop (the CSS recreations use `saturate(1.9)`) is NOT in the real recipe and was removed.
- **What lies behind (done, same day):** DWM blurs the real content behind a window, so the frames and content of other windows show, blurred, through the glass. The scene graph cannot blur what it draws, so `src/compositor/glass_scene.cpp` builds the backdrop itself, per frame part, at a quarter size: the blurred wallpaper, then the windows below in stacking order (bottom to top: always-on-top and pinned layers above the rest), each as its frame colour (`kit::frame_tint`) and its content (the few pixels that lie under the glass, read back from the client's texture with `wlr_texture_read_pixels` and averaged down), then a three-pass box blur of radius 3 (about 14 px at full size). The compositor shows that picture under the translucent frame instead of the plain wallpaper (`View::dyn_`, 32 px of margin so a window can move a little before a rebuild). It is rebuilt when something under the glass changed (`glass_backdrop_key`: the windows below that touch it, their boxes, a commit counter, focus), at most every 45 ms (`Server::mark_glass_dirty`); with nothing but the wallpaper under a window the key is 0 and the exact plain frost is used, so the common case costs nothing. Measured on the pixman rig (dev VM): a rebuild of all four parts with two windows underneath takes 1.6 ms; dragging a window over two others rebuilds about 18 times a second, 8 ms per 0.27 s drag, about 3% of one core; `commit_ms` stayed at 0.95-1.0 ms. The cost on the Celeron (GLES2 read-back stalls the GPU a little) is not measured.
- **Limits:** the content is a read-back of the client's texture (surfaces with a buffer scale other than 1 and windows whose texture cannot be read show only their frame colour); popups, layer-surface windows (the taskbar, menus) and desktop icons are not drawn into the backdrop; a window's own frame under another is its flat glass colour, not its own blurred frame.
